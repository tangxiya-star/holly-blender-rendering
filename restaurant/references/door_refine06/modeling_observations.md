# Door reference observations

Exact user share resolves to the Eatweek Guide photograph saved here. The real cast-bronze door is much darker in the 2024 user photo than in the bright Dinesen photo. No single-photo color is calibrated material albedo.

1. **Mass, frame and relief hierarchy.** Two narrow tall leaves, shallow upper transom plus three deeper narrative stories. Thin continuous center seam, clean stepped perimeter molding, four metal hinges per side. The relief has large smooth islands of backing visible between the trees; do not fill every square centimeter with noise.
2. **Large asymmetric tree composition.** Left upper story contains an oval horizontal ring knot, right upper story contains a branching web. Middle-left has a large smooth oval recess to the right of its trunk; middle-right has a broad S trunk. Bottom trunks bend in opposed but non-mirrored directions, with intersecting roots. These are low-to-high reliefs fused to a backing, not free round tree tubes stuck to a board.
3. **Real undercuts and directed scales.** Deep cavities beneath overlapping branches and roots produce the defining real shadows in the CHART sunlit photo. Coarse bark is layered, flaky and directionally aligned along bends. Model the silhouette, branch crossings, deep grooves and lamellar crests; reserve shader bump for submillimeter casting pits and fine cracks. Avoid uniform rope-like parallel strands.
4. **Patina and exposure.** Charcoal gray to gray-brown bronze dominates, warm desaturated exposed crests and dull cooler cavities. Small smooth egg-like shapes have tighter highlights. Broad mottled backing has subdued gloss. Do not make all bronze gold, or use copied photographic shadows as displacement depths. Roughness should vary by exposed crests versus oxidation, not just isotropic noise.
5. **Installed context and lighting.** Light washed irregular brick and dark steel surround provide scale and tone contrast. Door rests behind a thick concrete landing, three small circular uplights and two shallow steps; the supplied Eatweek image has a warm linear strip beneath the front step edge. Overcast front light is soft but still preserves crevice shadows; CHART direct sunlight is a useful secondary shape check. Render a 3/4 camera to show real relief depth as well as the reference-matched front hero.

Scale: CHART artist biography explicitly says the Alchemist door is 4 meters tall (https://chartartfair.com/journal/chart-in-tivoli/maria-rubinke). CHART door article and Dinesen say two tonnes collectively, not per leaf. The secondary Gambero Rosso three-square-meter wording is inconsistent and should not be used. Approximate two-leaf width from front photos is 2.5-2.6m at 4m height; this is image-derived, not measured architectural evidence. Gallery bio also describes a 4m tall polar bear, so do not mistakenly use that sentence as door evidence.
